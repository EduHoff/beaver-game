# BEAVER

## 1. The Object of the Game

In Beaver, each player attempts to build the longest possible sequence of cards on the table. The core challenge is not just emptying your hand, but managing the risk of exceeding the card limit or falling into an inescapable loop before you can successfully stop.

## 2. Basic Concepts and Setup

**Components**: The game is designed for standard fifty-two card decks, removing all jokers. It can also be played with any type of card or improvised pieces, provided they meet two basic requirements. The first is that it must be possible to clearly distinguish two groups, where value zero is represented by the black cards of clubs and spades, and value one by the red cards of hearts and diamonds. The second requirement is that all pieces must have identical backs, preventing players from identifying a card's value before revealing it or during blind interactions.

**Scalability and Balance**: You can combine as many decks or sets as you wish to accommodate your group. For a balanced experience, it is recommended to maintain exactly the same quantity of zero and one cards and to use one deck for every group of up to six players.

**Starting Hand and Mulligan**: Each player receives six cards to form their secret hand, while the remaining cards form the draw pile. If a player receives exclusively black cards in the initial distribution, they have the right to perform a mulligan. To do this, revealing the hand is mandatory: the player must immediately show them to the table to prove the condition, return them to the draw pile, shuffle, and draw a new hand of six cards. Since the game has not yet begun, if multiple players need to perform a mulligan simultaneously, the group may execute it in any way they prefer, for example, having all affected players reveal and return their cards to the deck at the same time for a single shuffle before redrawing. The procedure repeats whenever a newly drawn hand consists exclusively of black cards.

However, a player is free to bluff and choose not to reveal their hand, fully assuming the high risk of this choice. By doing so, it is mandatorily considered that the hand possesses at least one red card, ending the mulligan. If the player does not actually possess red cards and decides to conceal this fact, they will bear all mechanical consequences in the match (including the risk of suffering a collapse). Ignorance of the rule does not serve as a justification to avoid penalties, and there is no right to complain or demand a hand reshuffle afterward.

**Hand Limit**: A player can safely hold a maximum of twelve cards in their secret hand. The verification of this limit occurs exclusively in the interval between turns, after the complete resolution of all effects, cascades, and pending penalties from the concluded turn and before the start of the next turn. If, at that moment, the player holds thirteen or more cards, they suffer a hand burst, known as overflow, as detailed in Section 4.

**The Sequence**: This is the public area on the table where each player builds their face-up row of cards, visible to all participants. At the start of the game, the public sequence of all players begins empty. Whenever a penalty requires the removal of cards from a public sequence, the sweep will proceed from left to right, meaning from the oldest card to the most recent, respecting any specific removal priorities established by the rule itself.

**Eligible Player**: A player is considered eligible as long as they remain active in the match. Players who have already reached a stop or who have suffered a collapse are no longer eligible and are automatically skipped in the turn order during subsequent turns, as they lose possession of the turn and no longer participate in active dynamics.

**Turn Order**: The choice of who goes first and the direction of the turns, whether clockwise or counter-clockwise, are up to the players. Once defined, the order cannot be changed during an ongoing game. In multiple matches, it is recommended to alternate the starting player each round until everyone at the table has started first.

## 3. Turn Dynamics

Following the established order and automatically skipping participants who are no longer eligible, on their turn each active player is forced to play one card from their secret hand to the right end of their public sequence. The effect of the play is determined exclusively by the transition between the last card that was already on the table and the newly played card:

**Empty Row**: Whenever a player's public sequence is empty and they play a zero or one value card, nothing happens. The card simply starts the new row. This exemption applies both at the start of the match and after having the row wiped out by subsequent events.

**Black followed by Black (0 → 0)**: The player takes back the first card of the public row, which is the oldest one on the left, into their secret hand and immediately plays one more card into the sequence. The newly played card normally triggers a new transition with the previous card in the row, potentially setting off successive chain effects in the same turn, generating new episodes of zero followed by zero. The turn only ends when a transition finishes without requiring new plays or when the player triggers a collapse. If the secret hand becomes empty during the resolution of effects, the stop will only be verified after the conclusion of all ongoing effects resulting from the cards played in that turn.

**Black followed by Red (0 → 1)**: The active player chooses an eligible participant to draw cards from the draw pile. If they choose another eligible player, that opponent draws three cards. If they choose themselves, or if you are the only eligible player remaining at the table, you are forced to draw six cards.

**Red followed by Black (1 → 0)**: The active player chooses an eligible participant, with the option to choose themselves, and demands that they discard one red card from their secret hand. If they choose another player and that player does not possess red cards or chooses to bluff by hiding them, the active player blindly chooses two cards from that opponent's secret hand to be discarded. If they have only one card remaining, they discard that single card. If they choose themselves and do not possess red cards or choose to bluff, they must choose another eligible player to blindly select two cards from their (the active player's) secret hand to be discarded. If they have only one card remaining, they discard that single card. If the active player is the only eligible player remaining at the table with no other targets, they must choose themselves as the target and, if they do not possess red cards or choose to bluff, they must shuffle their own secret hand and blindly remove two cards, discarding the single remaining card if they possess only one.

**Red followed by Red (1 → 1)**: The active player chooses which color the next eligible player in the turn order will be forced to play on their turn. This requirement applies exclusively to the next eligible player, not extending to subsequent participants. If the next player does not play the required color, either because they do not have it or due to a tactical choice, they suffer a penalty before resolving the effect of the card they played. The choice of penalty belongs to the player who established the color requirement on the previous turn, even if they have already achieved a stop. This effect takes precedence over the effect of the newly played card, which will only be resolved after the complete application of the penalty. The enforcer chooses one of two possible penalties, the first being to blindly discard two cards from the offender's secret hand, chosen by the very player who set the color requirement, or to discard whatever they have if the offender possesses fewer than two cards. The second option is to force them to draw two cards from the draw pile. If you are the only eligible player remaining at the table, nothing happens.

**Special Rule for Three Consecutive Reds (1 → 1 → 1)**: Whenever the right end of your public sequence accumulates three or more consecutive red cards, this rule overrides the normal effect of one followed by one, even if you are the only eligible player remaining. You must choose one of the available options. The first option is to discard three red cards from your own secret hand. If you do not possess three red cards in hand or choose to bluff, the second option consists of mandatorily removing four cards from your public sequence and drawing four cards from the draw pile. If the sequence has fewer than four cards available, remove all existing cards. For this removal, scan the row from left to right, removing red cards first, from the oldest to the most recent. If there are not four red cards available, continue the removal with the remaining black cards, also following the left-to-right order. As long as you continue playing consecutive red cards in the same sequence, this punishment will continue to be applied to each play.

**Recycling and Card Depletion**: All cards discarded by transition effects or overflow go to the discard pile. If the draw pile runs out, shuffle the discard pile to form a new deck. If a player needs to draw cards, but both the draw pile and the discard pile are simultaneously empty, it is recommended to add new equivalent cards while maintaining the balanced proportion between zero and one. If there are no more cards available to restock the game, the match is immediately suspended due to a lack of resources, ending with no winners.

## 4. Critical Events and End of the Line

During the match, players are subject to three special states:

**Overflow**: Occurs exclusively after the complete conclusion of all effects and penalties of a turn, immediately before the next turn begins. If multiple players reach the hand burst condition simultaneously during this interval, the order of application of penalties among them is irrelevant to the final outcome. The penalty is applied solely if the player's secret hand contains thirteen or more cards at that exact moment of verification. Temporarily exceeding the twelve-card limit during the resolution of plays is not enough to trigger overflow, provided the player manages to reduce their hand to twelve or less before the end of all turn effects. If the player actually ends the turn with thirteen or more cards, they suffer two penalties in order. First, they lose all red cards from their public sequence, or they lose all black cards from the public sequence if they have no red cards on the table, wiping the row clean. Next, their secret hand must be reduced to exactly three cards. You choose an eligible opponent to blindly select which three cards will remain with you, discarding the rest. If you are the only eligible player remaining, shuffle your secret hand, blindly separate three cards, and discard the rest.

**Stop**: Happens at the end of any turn, following the complete resolution of all ongoing effects resulting from the cards played in that turn, if a player's secret hand is completely empty. This includes situations where the player empties their hand during their own turn or when another player forces them to discard their last card. The player ceases to be eligible and their public sequence remains intact on the table until the end of the match for victory tallying, and cannot be modified.

**Collapse**: Occurs when the player verifiably enters an inescapable mechanical loop. The starting milestone of this condition occurs at the exact instant the player's public sequence becomes composed exclusively of black cards, whether from the start of the turn or after removing the last red cards from the table during a cascade. From that moment on, the count starts from zero, and if the player plays three additional consecutive black cards still within the same cascade effect, even if they possess red cards in their secret hand, they suffer a collapse immediately. This rule prevents the player from intentionally prolonging the infinite cycle. The player is eliminated from the round, ceases to be eligible, and all of their cards from the public sequence and the secret hand go to the discard pile.

## 5. Victory and Tie-Breaker Criteria

When all players have achieved a stop or suffered a collapse, the match is ended.

**Collective Defeat**: If all players suffer a collapse, everyone loses the match. There are no winners nor tie-breaker criteria.

**Victory Tally**: This analysis occurs only among those who achieved a stop. The first criterion is the longest sequence, with the winner being whoever possesses the greatest quantity of cards on the table after the stop. The second criterion is the red majority, where in the event of a tie in sequence size, the winner is whoever has the greatest quantity of red cards in it. The third criterion is speed, where if the tie persists, the one who achieved the stop first wins. If two or more players achieve a stop simultaneously after the resolution of effects from the same turn, priority is given to the one whose secret hand was emptied by the effect activated by the active player. To avoid doubts or memory disputes at the end of the game, it is recommended that players keep some simple record, such as writing on a paper or using numbered markers to mark the exact order in which each participant achieved a stop.

## 6. Optional Rules of Fair Play and Conduct

The spirit of Beaver is based on deduction, tactical risk management, and honesty during hidden interactions. As a recommendation to preserve a healthy and fun environment, participants may agree upon additional rules of good conduct. Actions considered inconvenient or unfair, such as attempts to manipulate the deck outside the official rules, intentionally exposing private cards, or adopting bad-faith postures, may be judged and penalized by the group.

This rulebook does not intend to foresee, detail, or decide all possible situations, remaining intentionally vague in this aspect to prioritize the fun and adaptability of the table. Depending on the severity of the conduct, the assembled players have total freedom to define in consensus the punishments they deem proportional for the moment, whether by disqualifying the offender (making them immediately ineligible with direct elimination) or by defining severe resource losses on the table, as the group finds most appropriate for the situation. In the case of disqualification and elimination of a player, the operational destination of their cards (both from the secret hand and the public sequence) is also at the group's discretion, but the official recommendation is that they be sent to the discard pile, ensuring they normally participate in the card recycling process if the draw pile runs out.
